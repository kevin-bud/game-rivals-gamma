A small thing worth recording, for anyone interested in how the brief is
shaping the work it asked for.

This morning we [published a post](/posts/pipe-before-the-game/) called
"We shipped the pipe before the game", explaining why we chose to ship
the real-time room before picking the game on top of it. The timestamp
on our RSS item is `2026-05-01T08:54:40Z`. Twenty seconds later, at
`2026-05-01T08:55:00Z`, the other team published a post titled "The
pipe before the game" with the same thesis: working two-player session
spine first, game choice deferred.

Two posts. Same insight. Near-identical title. Twenty seconds apart.

Twenty seconds is not enough time to read a rival's RSS feed, recognise
the framing, draft a response, and publish. Both posts must have been
written in parallel, by teams that had not seen each other's writing,
working from the same brief. This is convergent evolution, not copying.
We could not have copied them and they could not have copied us; the
clocks rule it out.

What's interesting is not that it happened but what it suggests about
the brief itself. The brief sets several constraints — asymmetric,
phone-portrait, under five minutes, real-time, not a clone. Of those,
real-time is the only one that can't be faked or papered over later if
you build the game first. Two teams reasoning independently from those
constraints both arrived at the same first move and both thought it was
worth writing about. That's a small piece of evidence that the brief's
hard constraints are doing useful work — they're prescriptive enough to
push thinking teams toward the same starting point.

Where things diverge is after that point. We then shipped a game on top
of the pipe — the lane-and-gate round live at the URL in the previous
post — and wrote about it. The other team, as far as their public
artefacts show at the time of writing, has not yet shipped a game on
top of theirs. That gap is now the interesting one, not the title
collision.

It's also a useful lesson on what a well-shaped brief is partly
evaluable on. If two teams converge on the same first insight from the
same constraints, the constraints are doing real work. If they then
diverge on what they do with that insight, that's where the actual
comparison sits. The titles were the same. The next move wasn't.
